## TL;DR

We are building a 112 page Halloween maze book for children aged roughly 6 to 10, and more importantly we are building the **code that generates it**. Fifty mazes follow one character, Jim, through a single trick or treat night. Each maze has a twist that ordinary maze books do not have: the goal is not just to reach the exit, it is to reach the exit **collecting the most candy**, which means the maze must contain several possible routes and the child has to choose between them.

That twist has one critical technical consequence that drives the entire generator design: **a standard "perfect" maze will not work.** Perfect mazes have exactly one route between any two points, so there is nothing to choose. We need braided mazes with a controlled number of loops, plus a candy placement algorithm that guarantees one route is strictly better than all the others.

Everything is generated from code: mazes, icons placed on the grid, page layout, story pages, solutions section, and the final print ready PDF. The only hand made inputs are about eighteen small SVG icons and fifty short lines of story text. Once it works, retargeting it to Christmas, Easter, dinosaurs, space or any other theme costs about a day. **The generator is the asset. The Halloween book is the first output.**

Estimated cost to produce book one: **40 to 60 euros** in tooling plus one proof copy. Target list price **9.99 USD / 9.99 EUR / 7.99 GBP**, which sits exactly on Amazon's 60 percent royalty threshold and yields roughly **4.25 USD per copy sold**.

### Why this concept is worth building

1. **Amazon does not classify maze and puzzle books as "low content."** They avoid the restrictions that apply to journals, planners and notebooks, and they are eligible for a free KDP ISBN, Look Inside, and series pages.
2. **No drawing skill is required.** Mazes are generated by algorithm, not drawn. The only art is about eighteen tiny icons.
3. **The candy mechanic is a genuine differentiator.** Almost every maze book on Amazon is "start here, finish there." A scoring layer gives the listing something real to say in the first bullet of the description, and gives reviewers something to praise.
4. **Braided mazes with a best route are self checking.** A child can compare their score against the answer key, which parents like.
5. **The story wrapper turns 50 unrelated puzzles into a book**, which justifies the price and supports a series.

### Explicit non goals for v1

- No colour interior. Colour printing roughly triples the print cost and destroys the margin.
- No illustrated scene art. Story pages are text plus one small icon.
- No hexagonal, circular or irregular mazes. Square grid only.
- No ebook edition. Mazes are a paper product.

## 2. Core puzzle mechanic

### 2.1 Rules as printed in the book

> **How to play**
>
> 1. Jim starts in the corner with the arrow. The candy bucket is the finish.
> 2. Draw one line from Jim to the bucket.
> 3. Never cross your own line and never go back down a path you already used.
> 4. Every sweet your line runs through is one you collect.
> 5. A pumpkin means a dead end. There is no way through.
> 6. Count your sweets and write the number in the box.
> 7. The answers at the back show the best route and the top score. Can you match it?

### 2.2 Why the maze must be braided

This is the single most important design decision in the document.

A **perfect maze** (the output of recursive backtracking, Prim's, Kruskal's and most textbook algorithms) is a spanning tree. There is exactly one simple path between any two cells. With only one possible route, "collect the most candy" has no meaning, because the player has no choice at all.

A **braided maze** has some walls removed to create loops. With loops there are several genuinely different simple paths from start to finish, and choosing between them is the puzzle.

We therefore generate a perfect maze first and then **braid it deliberately**, adding a controlled number of loops. The number of extra passages beyond the spanning tree is the maze's cyclomatic number, `k`. Two competing pressures set the value of `k`:

- `k` too low: too few routes, puzzle is trivial.
- `k` too high: the number of simple paths explodes combinatorially, the maze looks like an open field rather than a maze, and our validation step becomes slow.

**Specification: keep `k` between 3 and 12, scaling with difficulty.** This keeps the count of simple start to finish paths in the low thousands, which we can enumerate exhaustively in well under a second.

### 2.3 Dead ends and pumpkins

We do **not** braid away every dead end. Dead ends are visually what makes something read as a maze, and they give us somewhere to put pumpkins.

- Target 4 to 10 remaining dead ends per maze, scaling with difficulty.
- Every remaining dead end cell gets a pumpkin icon.
- **Hard rule: never place a candy in a dead end cell.** Under rule 3 (no retracing) a candy in a dead end is unreachable, which is an unfair trap and will generate one star reviews.

### 2.4 Constraints that make the puzzle good

The candy placement must satisfy all of these, or the maze is rejected and regenerated:

| ID | Constraint | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Exactly one simple path achieves the maximum candy count | The answer key must be unambiguous |
| C2 | Best score minus second best score is at least `margin` (2 in acts 1 to 3, 1 in acts 4 to 5) | Near ties feel arbitrary to a child |
| C3 | The shortest path is **not** the best path | Otherwise the puzzle is just a normal maze |
| C4 | The best path is at most 1.6x the length of the shortest path | Otherwise it is tedious to draw |
| C5 | At least 3 distinct simple paths collect 60 percent or more of the maximum | The wrong answers must be tempting |
| C6 | No candy on the start cell, the goal cell, or any dead end cell | Fairness and clarity |
| C7 | No two candies adjacent in the same corridor run | Reads as a cluster, wastes the grid |
| C8 | Total candies within the act's target range | Consistent difficulty |

## 3. Difficulty model

The maze square on the page is fixed at **6.75 x 6.75 inches**. Cell size is therefore `6.75 / n` for an `n x n` grid. The floor for a child drawing with a pencil is about 0.375 in (9.5 mm), which caps us at 18 x 18.

| Act | Mazes | Grid | Cell size | Candies | Loops `k` | Dead ends | Margin | Wall stroke |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Getting Ready | 1 to 10 | 8 x 8 | 0.844 in | 5 to 7 | 3 to 4 | 4 to 5 | 2 | 3.0 pt |
| 2. The Neighbourhood | 11 to 20 | 10 x 10 | 0.675 in | 7 to 9 | 4 to 5 | 5 to 6 | 2 | 3.0 pt |
| 3. The Dark End of the Street | 21 to 30 | 12 x 12 | 0.563 in | 9 to 11 | 5 to 6 | 6 to 7 | 2 | 2.75 pt |
| 4. The Haunted Half Hour | 31 to 40 | 14 x 14 | 0.482 in | 11 to 13 | 6 to 8 | 7 to 8 | 1 | 2.5 pt |
| 5. The Long Way Home | 41 to 48 | 16 x 16 | 0.422 in | 13 to 15 | 8 to 10 | 8 to 9 | 1 | 2.5 pt |
| Finale | 49 to 50 | 18 x 18 | 0.375 in | 16 to 18 | 10 to 12 | 9 to 10 | 1 | 2.25 pt |

Start cell is always the top left region, goal cell always the bottom right region, with a small random offset so every page does not look identical. Specifically: start is drawn uniformly from cells within the top left 3 x 3 block, goal from the bottom right 3 x 3 block.

### 4.3 Story generation notes

Write these yourself or edit AI drafts heavily. Two reasons. First, fifty short lines is under an hour of work. Second, it lets you honestly answer "no" to the AI generated **text** question at upload, which keeps your disclosure simple. If you do have AI draft them, declare it. Do not guess at the line, and do not let anybody talk you into "I edited it so it counts as assisted." Amazon's own wording is explicit that content created by an AI tool counts as AI generated even if you edit it substantially afterwards.

Voice rules for consistency:
- Present tense, second person imperative where possible ("Find a way through").
- Never name a brand of sweet.
- Never describe anything actually threatening happening to Jim or Ben.
- Keep vocabulary at roughly age 7 reading level.
- Choose one English variant and hold it. The beat sheet above is British ("sweets", "pavement", "torch"). **For the US market as primary, convert to American English**: sweets to candy, pavement to sidewalk, torch to flashlight, Mum to Mom. Keep two `story.yaml` files, one per variant.

### 5.2 Hard technical requirements for every icon

These exist because the smallest icons render at about **5.5 mm** on the 18 x 18 finale mazes. Most AI generated line art is unusable at that size.

- **Format:** SVG 1.1, plain SVG (not Inkscape SVG), `viewBox="0 0 100 100"`.
- **Composition:** artwork centred, with at least 6 units of empty padding on all four sides.
- **Colour:** pure black `#000000` only. No greys, no gradients, no `opacity`, no `fill-opacity`.
- **Geometry:** all strokes converted to filled paths. **No `stroke` attributes anywhere.** If you leave live strokes, the line weight will not scale with the icon and your 5.5 mm icons will turn into black blobs while the 0.6 in icons look spindly.
- **Minimum feature size:** no line, gap or hole thinner than **7 units** in the 100 unit viewBox. At 5.5 mm render that is 0.39 mm, which is the practical floor for print on demand paper.
- **Complexity ceiling:** 12 or fewer distinct subpaths per icon.
- **No text** inside any icon.
- **No background rectangle.** Transparent background.

**Acceptance test for every icon:** render it at 5.5 mm, print it on a normal home printer, hold the page at arm's length. If you cannot instantly say what it is, it fails. Simplify and repeat. This test will reject most first attempts and that is the point.

### 5.3 Asset production pipeline

You have no design skills, so use this chain:

1. **Generate** a 1024 x 1024 PNG. Prompt template:
   > `simple black and white icon of a [SUBJECT], thick uniform outline, bold chunky shapes, flat vector style, pure black on pure white background, no shading, no gradient, no grey, no texture, centered, minimal detail, high contrast, coloring book icon`

   Negative prompt: `shading, gradient, grey, greyscale, texture, realistic, 3d, photo, text, letters, watermark, background, frame, border, thin lines, fine detail`

   Generate 8 to 12 candidates per icon. Pick on the "still readable when squinting" criterion, not on which is prettiest.

2. **Vectorise.** Vectorizer.ai (about 10 euros for one month, cancel after) or Adobe Illustrator Image Trace using the Black and White Logo preset. Threshold hard so nothing grey survives.

3. **Clean in Inkscape** (free):
   - `Path > Union` to merge fragments.
   - `Path > Simplify` once, then check you have not lost the silhouette.
   - `Edit > Select All`, then `Path > Object to Path` to kill any remaining strokes.
   - Check the XML editor for any `fill` that is not `#000000`.
   - `File > Clean Up Document`, then save as **Plain SVG**.
   - Resize the document to a 100 x 100 unit viewBox.

4. **Validate** with `src/jimmaze/validate_assets.py`, which should fail the build on: any non black fill, any `stroke` attribute, any feature under 7 units, more than 12 subpaths, or missing padding.

**Budget roughly two full evenings for all eighteen icons.** It will take longer than you expect because of the 5.5 mm test.

### 5.4 Disclosure consequence

If the icons originate from an AI image generator, then when you upload you must answer **yes** to the AI generated images question. Vectorising and cleaning does not change that. This does not block publication and is not shown to customers, but failing to declare it is one of the more common causes of blocked books and terminated accounts.

## 6. Maze generator specification

### 6.1 Prior art to review on GitHub

Do not write the base generation algorithms from scratch. Review these first:

- **`john-science/mazelib`** (`https://github.com/john-science/mazelib`), MIT licensed, pip installable. The strongest candidate. It provides generating algorithms (Prim's, Kruskal's, Recursive Backtracker, Aldous Broder, Hunt and Kill, Wilson's), **transmuting algorithms which is where its braiding lives**, and solvers that explicitly handle imperfect mazes with loops. Read `docs/MAZE_GEN_ALGOS.md`, `docs/MAZE_SOLVE_ALGOS.md` and `docs/API.md`.
- **Jamis Buck's maze algorithm writeups** and the book *Mazes for Programmers*. The clearest explanations of braiding, dead end culling, and the "river" property that controls whether corridors are long and snaky or short and choppy. Long corridors read better for young children.
- **`KjeldSchmidt/Python-Maze-Generator`** (`https://github.com/KjeldSchmidt/Python-Maze-Generator`), a minimal readable backtracker if you want to understand the core loop before pulling in a dependency.

Note that several other repositories named `mazelib` exist, including a public domain C library at `blastbay/mazelib` and various forks. The Python one you want is `john-science/mazelib`.

**Recommendation:** use `mazelib` for step 1 (perfect maze generation) and write steps 2 to 6 yourself, because the candy logic is the actual product and no library will do it.

### 6.2 Data model

```python
@dataclass
class Cell:
    row: int
    col: int
    walls: dict[str, bool]   # {"N": True, "E": True, "S": True, "W": True}

@dataclass
class Maze:
    scene_id: int            # 1..50
    act: int                 # 1..5
    size: int                # n, grid is n x n
    seed: int
    cells: list[list[Cell]]
    start: tuple[int, int]
    goal: tuple[int, int]
    candies: dict[tuple[int, int], str]   # cell -> icon variant id
    pumpkins: list[tuple[int, int]]
    best_path: list[tuple[int, int]]
    best_score: int
    second_best_score: int
    shortest_path_len: int
    best_path_len: int
    total_simple_paths: int
```

Serialise to `out/mazes/{scene_id:02d}.json`. **Every maze must be reproducible from its seed alone.** Seed with `hash((BOOK_ID, scene_id))` so that rerunning the build six months later, or fixing one broken maze, does not reshuffle the other forty nine.

### 6.3 Generation algorithm

```
generate(scene_id, act_config):
    for attempt in 1..MAX_ATTEMPTS (500):
        seed = derive_seed(BOOK_ID, scene_id, attempt)

        # Step 1: perfect maze
        grid = recursive_backtracker(n, seed)        # long corridors, low junction density

        # Step 2: pick start and goal
        start = random cell in top-left 3x3 block
        goal  = random cell in bottom-right 3x3 block

        # Step 3: braid to target cyclomatic number k
        #   repeatedly remove a wall between two cells that are currently
        #   far apart in the maze graph (high tree distance), which creates
        #   a meaningful loop rather than a useless 2x2 pocket
        k = random int in act_config.k_range
        braid_to_k(grid, k, min_loop_length=6)

        # Step 4: cull or keep dead ends to hit target count
        dead_ends = find_dead_ends(grid)
        adjust_dead_ends(grid, act_config.dead_end_range)

        # Step 5: enumerate all simple start->goal paths
        paths = enumerate_simple_paths(grid, start, goal, cap=200_000)
        if paths is CAP_EXCEEDED: continue          # too open, retry
        if len(paths) < 8: continue                 # too closed, retry

        # Step 6: place candies satisfying C1..C8
        placement = place_candies(grid, paths, start, goal, dead_ends, act_config)
        if placement is None: continue

        # Step 7: place pumpkins on every remaining dead end
        pumpkins = dead_ends

        # Step 8: validate
        if not validate(maze): continue
        return maze

    raise GenerationFailure(scene_id)
```

### 6.4 Path enumeration

Plain depth first search over cells, maintaining a visited set, emitting the path on reaching the goal. Because `k` is capped at 12, the number of simple paths stays tractable. Add a hard cap of 200,000 paths and a 2 second timeout per maze; if either trips, discard and regenerate with a new seed. This is cheaper than trying to be clever.

Precompute, per path, a bitmask of visited cells. Scoring a candy placement then becomes a popcount over the intersection of the placement mask and the path mask, which makes step 6 fast enough to brute force.

### 6.5 Candy placement

```
place_candies(grid, paths, start, goal, dead_ends, cfg):
    forbidden = {start, goal} | set(dead_ends)
    candidates = all cells on at least one path, minus forbidden

    for trial in 1..3000:
        m = random int in cfg.candy_range
        chosen = weighted_sample(candidates, m)
            # weight cells by how many DIFFERENT paths cross them,
            # favouring mid-frequency cells: cells on every path are
            # boring (free candy), cells on one path are too punishing

        if violates_C7_adjacency(chosen): continue

        scores = sorted([score(path, chosen) for path in paths], reverse=True)
        best, second = scores[0], scores[1]

        if count_of(scores, best) != 1: continue            # C1
        if best - second < cfg.margin: continue             # C2

        best_path = argmax path
        if best_path is a shortest path: continue           # C3
        if len(best_path) > 1.6 * shortest_len: continue    # C4
        if count(s >= 0.6*best for s in scores) < 3: continue # C5

        return chosen

    return None
```

The weighting in `weighted_sample` is the part that makes the puzzle feel designed rather than random. Cells crossed by roughly 30 to 70 percent of all paths are the interesting ones, because they represent real decisions. Build a histogram of path frequency per cell and sample mostly from that band, with a few from the low frequency band to reward exploration.

### 6.6 Validation and preflight per maze

Fail the build loudly on any of these:

- Any C1 to C8 violation.
- Best path passes within one cell of the page edge for more than half its length (looks lazy).
- More than 35 percent of cells unreachable from start (disconnected region).
- Any candy on a cell the best path does not physically fit through.
- Icon overlap: two icons in cells whose rendered bounding boxes intersect.
- Solution path renders as a line that visually merges with a wall (check minimum 2 pt clearance).

### 6.7 Contact sheet review

Generate `out/preview/contact_sheet.png` showing all 50 mazes at thumbnail size on one image. **A human must eyeball this before every build.** Algorithmic validation catches correctness, not ugliness. You are looking for mazes that are visually boring (large open areas, obvious straight shot to the goal, candy clustered in one corner) and regenerating those individually with a bumped attempt counter.

### 7.2 Page plan (112 pages)

**Critical parity rule:** the story page must always be the left hand page of an open spread, so the child sees the story and the maze together. Left hand pages are even numbered. Front matter is therefore **5 pages, an odd number**, so that scene 1's story lands on page 6. If you add or remove front matter you must add or remove it **two pages at a time** or the entire book flips and every story page ends up on the wrong side.

If you want act divider pages, they must be added in pairs for the same reason. Each pair costs 2 pages and about 0.04 USD of print cost. Four dividers would take the book to 120 pages.

```
|   Candies collected:  [ ][ ][ ][ ][ ] ...   |
|   Total: [    ]        Best possible: 11    |
|                                             |
|                                     [ 47 ]  |
+---------------------------------------------+
```

- Maze square 6.75 x 6.75 in, horizontally centred in the live area, top edge 0.75 in below the top margin.
- **Tally strip** directly below the maze, 6.75 in wide x 1.35 in tall: a row of small tick boxes (one per available candy, 0.28 in squares), plus a "Total" write in box 0.9 x 0.5 in and the printed "Best possible" number.
- Scene number in the outside bottom corner, 11 pt.
- Config flag `tally_position: "below" | "right"`. The `"right"` variant puts a 1.2 in vertical tally column beside the maze and shrinks the maze square to 5.9 in, which drops the finale cell size to 0.33 in. **Default to `"below"`, because maze size matters more than layout novelty.**

Printing the "Best possible" number on the page is deliberate. It converts a maze into a target, which is what makes a child attempt it twice.

### 7.4 Story page (verso, even)

```
+---------------------------------------------+
|  [web corner ornament]                      |
|                                             |
|                                             |
|                 47                          |
|            BEN'S GATE                       |
|                                             |
|     Drop Ben at his gate and split the      |
|     haul fairly. Mostly fairly.             |
|                                             |
|                 [jim icon]                  |
|                                             |
|                                             |
+---------------------------------------------+
```

- Scene number 28 pt, centred, 3.2 in from top.
- Title 34 pt, all caps, centred, chunky display face.
- Body 16 pt, centred, maximum two lines, generous 1.5 line spacing.
- One decorative icon at 0.6 in, centred below, cycled from the `deco_*` set by act.
- Web corner ornament in the outside top corner, story pages only.
- **Deliberately mostly empty.** White space is what makes a cheap two colour interior read as designed rather than thin.

### 7.5 Solutions pages

- 9 mazes per page in a 3 x 3 grid, each thumbnail 2.1 x 2.1 in.
- Best path drawn as a 1.5 pt solid line with rounded joins, clearly distinguishable from the 0.75 pt walls at thumbnail scale.
- Candies on the best path shown as small filled dots, not full icons (icons are illegible at this scale).
- Score printed under each: `47. Best: 14 candies`.
- Six pages covers 50 with room to spare.

### 7.6 Typography

- One display face for titles and numbers, one body face. Both must be licensed for commercial embedding. Open Font License faces from Google Fonts are fine and free, and OFL explicitly permits embedding.
- **Convert all text to outlines in the final PDF.** This eliminates every font embedding and licensing question at the KDP preflight stage and costs nothing since the text is static.
- Keep a non outlined build (`interior_editable.pdf`) for your own proofreading, because you cannot spellcheck outlines.

## 8. Build pipeline and repository

### 8.1 Stack

- **Python 3.11+**
- `mazelib` for base maze generation
- `numpy` for grid and bitmask operations
- **`reportlab`** for PDF output. Chosen over an SVG to PDF conversion chain because it gives exact control over page size in points, guarantees a single consistent PDF, and avoids a whole class of unit conversion bugs. Draw the maze directly as ReportLab path objects.
- `svglib` to convert the eighteen icon SVGs into ReportLab drawings once at startup, cached.
- `pypdf` or `qpdf` only if you need to merge or reorder afterwards
- `PyYAML` for config
- `Pillow` for the preview contact sheet

Avoid a browser or HTML based rendering path. Print PDFs from HTML introduce colour space and DPI problems that are painful to debug at proof stage.

### 8.4 Preflight checks before upload

| Check | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Page count | Exactly 112, even |
| Page size | Every page exactly 612 x 792 pt, no rotation flags |
| Fonts | Zero embedded fonts in `interior.pdf` (all outlined) |
| Colour space | DeviceGray only. No RGB, no CMYK, no spot colours |
| Ink | Pure black `0.0` gray, no tints below 100 percent anywhere |
| Transparency | Fully flattened, no transparency groups |
| Safe area | No mark within 0.25 in of any trim edge |
| Raster | No embedded raster images at all (vector only) |
| Parity | Page 6 and every subsequent even page is a story page |
| Cross check | Every printed "Best possible" equals the JSON `best_score` |

The last check has caught real bugs in every book project of this shape. Automate it.

## 9. KDP publishing specification

### 9.1 Print cost and royalty

US, 8.5 x 11 black ink on white paper, 112 pages. KDP charges a fixed 2.84 USD for large trim black and white books under 110 pages, and above 110 pages switches to 1.00 USD fixed plus 0.017 USD per page.

```
112 pages  ->  1.00 + (112 x 0.017)  =  2.904 USD print cost
List price 9.99 USD, at or above the 9.99 threshold, so 60 percent royalty
Royalty = 0.60 x (9.99 - 2.904) = 4.25 USD per copy
```

For comparison, the same book at 8.99 USD falls below the threshold and earns 50 percent: `0.50 x (8.99 - 2.904) = 3.04 USD`. **Pricing lower loses you 28 percent of your margin per sale.** Do not price below the threshold in any marketplace.

Dropping to 108 pages would get you back under the 110 page flat rate at 2.84 USD, worth about 0.04 USD per copy. Not worth losing two solutions pages over, but noted.

For EU marketplaces the print cost is roughly 2.48 EUR for the equivalent book and the royalty is calculated on the **VAT exclusive** price (German books carry 7 percent VAT), giving roughly `0.60 x (9.34 - 2.48) = 4.12 EUR`. Verify every marketplace in KDP's own printing cost calculator before you set prices, because these tables change.

### 9.2 Pricing across marketplaces

Set each price **at** the 60 percent threshold, never below:

| Marketplace | Price |
|---|---|
| amazon.com | 9.99 USD |
| amazon.co.uk | 7.99 GBP |
| amazon.de / .fr / .es / .it / .nl | 9.99 EUR |
| amazon.ca | 13.99 CAD |
| amazon.com.au | 13.99 AUD |

### 9.3 Cover specification

- Single flat PDF containing back cover, spine and front cover.
- **Spine width** = page count x paper thickness. White paper is 0.002252 in per page. `112 x 0.002252 = 0.2522 in`.
- **Full cover width** = 0.125 bleed + 8.5 back + 0.2522 spine + 8.5 front + 0.125 bleed = **17.5022 in**
- **Full cover height** = 0.125 + 11 + 0.125 = **11.25 in**
- Spine text is only permitted above 79 pages, so at 112 pages you can have it. Keep it 0.0625 in clear of the spine folds.
- Download the exact template from KDP's cover template generator rather than trusting these numbers. Any change to page count changes the spine and invalidates the cover.
- Cover must be CMYK or RGB raster at 300 DPI, or vector. Unlike the interior, the cover **is** colour and should be: orange, black, purple.
- Front cover must communicate in a 200 pixel thumbnail: big title, Jim, a maze fragment, candy. Test at thumbnail size before committing.

### 9.4 Metadata

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Title | Jim's Halloween Maze Adventure |
| Subtitle | 50 Candy Collecting Mazes for Kids Ages 6 to 10 |
| Series | Jim's Adventure Mazes (book 1) |
| Author | A consistent human sounding pen name, used across the series |
| Language | English |
| ISBN | Free KDP assigned (maze books are not low content, so you are eligible) |
| Reading age | 6 to 10 |
| Categories | Children's Books > Activities, Crafts & Games > Activity Books > Mazes; Children's Books > Holidays & Celebrations > Halloween |

**Seven keyword slots.** Use natural search phrases, not stuffed strings. Amazon's metadata guidelines prohibit keyword stuffing and misleading terms, and puzzle book listings get caught for it regularly.

1. halloween maze book for kids
2. halloween activity book ages 6 8
3. trick or treat puzzle book
4. maze books for kids ages 8 10
5. halloween gift for kids
6. halloween party favor books
7. mazes for kids with answers

**Never** put a trademarked term, a competing brand, a sweet brand, or a film character in any metadata field. This is one of the most common causes of blocked coloring and puzzle books.

**Description** should lead with the mechanic, because that is the differentiator:

> Every maze in this book has more than one way through. Only one of them collects the most candy. Follow Jim through fifty mazes on the best Halloween night of his life, from the attic costume hunt all the way to the house on the hill and the long walk home. Big, clear mazes get gradually trickier. Every page shows the best possible score so you know what you are aiming for, and full solutions are in the back.

### 9.5 AI content disclosure

At upload KDP asks separately about **text**, **images**, and **translations**.

- **Images:** if the icons came out of an AI generator, answer **yes**, even though you vectorised and cleaned them. The mazes themselves are algorithmically generated, not AI generated, and are not what the question is asking about.
- **Text:** answer honestly based on how you actually produced the fifty story lines.
- **Translations:** if you localise using AI, answer yes on those editions.

This disclosure is internal to Amazon, is not shown to buyers, and per KDP does not affect royalties or ranking. Getting it wrong is what causes problems.

## 10. German legal requirements for the imprint page

As an Einzelunternehmen publishing from Germany, page 2 of the interior must carry more than a copyright line.

- **GPSR (Regulation (EU) 2023/988), applicable since 13 December 2024.** Physical products sold into the EU must show the manufacturer's name, postal address and electronic address. Because you are established in the EU you can act as your own economic operator and do not need a third party EU Responsible Person. KDP does not add this for you. Put it on page 2.
- **Impressumspflicht.** A ladungsfähige Anschrift, meaning a real street address that can receive legal service. A Postfach does not satisfy this. If you do not want your home address in a few thousand paperbacks, budget for an Impressumsservice or a registered business address.
- **Deutsche Nationalbibliothek Pflichtexemplare.** Not handled by KDP. Print on demand titles are deposited electronically first, and once more than 25 copies have sold you must also deliver two printed copies to the DNB. Failing the print deposit obligation is an Ordnungswidrigkeit carrying a fine of up to 10,000 EUR.
- **Buchpreisbindung.** As self publisher you set the binding German list price. It does not block anything, it just fixes your 9.99 EUR.
- **US tax interview / W-8BEN.** Complete before your first sale. Under Article 12 of the US Germany tax treaty royalty withholding is 0 percent versus the 30 percent statutory rate. Your German tax ID is sufficient, no US TIN needed.

Suggested page 2 block:

```
Jim's Halloween Maze Adventure
First edition, 2026

Text and puzzle design: [pen name]
Published by: [legal name], [street], [postcode] [city], Germany
Contact: [email address]

Manufacturer and EU economic operator under Regulation (EU) 2023/988:
[legal name], [street], [postcode] [city], Germany, [email address]

All rights reserved.
Printed by Amazon.
```

## 11. Timeline

Halloween 2026 falls on Saturday 31 October. Halloween book buying on Amazon peaks roughly 1 to 24 October, and a new listing with no sales rank and no reviews needs two to three weeks of visibility before it converts organically. **You are already late. Publishing by 26 September is the realistic target and 10 October is the point past which this becomes a 2027 book.**

| Dates | Deliverable |
|---|---|
| 14 to 16 Sep | Generator: grid, braiding, path enumeration, candy placement, all 50 mazes passing validation. This is the hard part, do it first. |
| 17 to 19 Sep | Eighteen icons through the full pipeline, passing the 5.5 mm test. |
| 20 to 21 Sep | Page renderer, story text finalised, interior.pdf building end to end. |
| 22 Sep | Contact sheet review, regenerate ugly mazes, preflight passes. |
| 23 Sep | Cover. Order a proof copy. Complete KDP tax interview. |
| 24 Sep | Upload, set metadata and prices for all marketplaces, submit. |
| 25 to 27 Sep | KDP review (roughly 72 hours). Live. |
| 28 Sep onward | Amazon Ads at 5 EUR per day on exact match keywords. Proof copy arrives around 1 to 5 Oct. Fix anything the proof reveals and republish. |

**Deliberate risk being taken:** publishing before the proof copy arrives. Normally you never do this. Here the seasonal window justifies it, provided preflight passes and you have printed the interior at home on A4 to sanity check the maze sizes. If the proof shows a problem you republish, which costs a few days of ranking but not the season.

## 14. Open decisions

1. **English variant.** US market is bigger, so American English is probably primary. Decide before writing the fifty lines, not after.
2. **Pen name.** Must be human sounding and consistent across the series. Amazon flags non human author names.
3. **Tally position.** Spec defaults to below the maze. Print one page each way and decide with a pencil in hand.
4. **Act dividers.** Skipped in v1. Adding four costs 8 pages and about 0.14 USD per copy.
5. **Series naming.** "Jim's Adventure Mazes" lets you ship Christmas, Easter, Dinosaur and Space editions against the same character. Decide now, because renaming a live series is painful.
6. **Whether to also sell a digital printable version.** The same generator can emit a 50 page PDF for Etsy or Gumroad at around 90 percent margin. Strongly worth doing once the KDP edition is live, and it costs one make target.
